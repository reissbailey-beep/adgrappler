My story

Most gym owners learn marketing the hard way. I came at it backwards.

Hi, I’m Reiss Bailey.

I run New School BJJ Brixton in south London. I coach on the mats every week, and I still compete at black belt.

But before I owned a gym, I ran an ads agency. It was called Ad Grappler.

Most gym owners open the doors first and work out the marketing later. Boosted posts, flyers, a free week, whatever a mate says worked for them. They learn it the hard way, with their own money.

I got to skip that bit. When I opened the Brixton club I already knew how to run ads, build automations and set up a pipeline. So I did all of it from day one, and pointed the whole machine at my own gym.

What happened

We started out running classes in Street Gym. We had 120 members in our first year, and after that first year we moved into our own place at 130 Brixton Hill.

Since then we’ve kept expanding and upgrading the space. Within three years we were at around 300 members.

Our Instagram and Facebook lead ads have brought in leads at £6–8 each, consistently, year after year. In the last 30 days alone we got 133 leads for £908.

Same ads. Same pipeline. Same follow-up. It’s running right now while you read this.

The wall I watched other gyms hit

Meanwhile, I watched friends in the BJJ scene go through something I never had to.

Good coaches. Good rooms. They’d put money into ads and the enquiries would come in. Mid-class. Or at 10pm on a Sunday.

Nobody replied for a day, because they were on the mat or asleep. By then that person had booked somewhere else, or talked themselves out of it.

They paid for the lead. Then they lost it. And a lot of them decided ads just don’t work.

Getting the lead is half the job. Getting them a reply before they go cold is the other half.

The ads were never really the problem. It’s what happens in the minutes after someone taps “Submit”.

At my gym that bit runs itself. A text goes out within minutes, day or night. An assistant answers their questions and books the trial. Reminders make sure they turn up. And one pipeline shows me every lead from first click to paying member.

Why I’m bringing Ad Grappler back

So I’m relaunching Ad Grappler, for gyms and nothing else.

It’s the exact machine my gym runs on, built inside your own accounts. Three gyms to start, one per area.

And because the follow-up doesn’t depend on me picking up my phone, I still get to train and compete. This year I won the IBJJF World Masters at black belt, four fights to gold, two years after winning it at brown belt. The gym doesn’t stop when I’m away competing, and neither does the follow-up.

Before you scroll on

You might be thinking one of these.

“Ads don’t work for gyms.”

My gym has run on them since day one. 133 leads last month at £6.83 each. Ads work fine. Ads with nobody following up are what don’t work.

“I tried boosting posts. Waste of money.”

Fair. A boosted post buys likes from people who were never going to train. A lead ad puts a short form in front of local people who want to try a class, and the second they submit it, the follow-up starts. Different tool, different result.

“I haven’t got time for another system.”

That’s the point of it. Your part is a 15-minute call and a kickoff form that takes about half an hour. We build the rest. After that, the texts, answers, bookings and reminders happen without you.

“My area’s different.”

It might be. That’s what the call is for. I’ll look at your offer, your area and how much room you’ve got on the mat, and tell you straight if I don’t think it’ll work. And because I only take one gym per area, I’ll never be helping the gym down the road compete with you for the same people.
